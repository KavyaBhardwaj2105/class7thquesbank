Section G — HOTS / Higher Order Thinking (10)

46. A teacher has 48 red beads and 72 blue beads. She wants to make the greatest possible number of identical packets, using all beads. How many packets can she make?

Correct Answer: 24 packets. HCF(48,72) = 24.

47. Three bells ring every 12, 18 and 30 minutes. If they ring together at 8:00 a.m., when will they next ring together?

Correct Answer: 9:00 a.m. LCM(12,18,30) = 180 minutes = 3 hours.

48. Two numbers have product 720 and HCF 6. If one number is 30, what is the other number?

Correct Answer: 24, because 720 ÷ 30 = 24 and HCF(30,24) = 6.

49. Find the smallest number greater than 100 that is divisible by both 12 and 15.

Correct Answer: 120. LCM(12,15) = 60, and the first multiple greater than 100 is 120.

50. A number is divisible by 4 and 9. Which of the following could be the number?

Correct Answer: 72. A number divisible by both 4 and 9 is divisible by LCM(4,9) = 36.

51. Two ropes of lengths 84 m and 126 m are to be cut into equal pieces of maximum possible length. What is the length of each piece?

Correct Answer: 42 m. HCF(84,126) = 42.

52. A student says, “If two numbers have LCM 60, both numbers must be factors of 60.” Is the statement correct?

Correct Answer: Yes. Each number whose LCM is 60 must divide 60.

53. Find the smallest positive integer that leaves no remainder when divided by 8, 12 and 15.

Correct Answer: 120. LCM(8,12,15) = 120.

54. The HCF of two numbers is 12. Which pair could represent the two numbers?

Correct Answer: 36 and 60. Their common factors include 12, and their greatest common factor is 12.

55. A school has 96 notebooks and 144 pencils. They want to distribute them into identical kits with no items left over. What is the greatest number of kits possible?

Correct Answer: 48 kits. HCF(96,144) = 48.
