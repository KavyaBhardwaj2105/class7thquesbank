📐 Chapter 14 — Constructions and Tilings

This chapter develops geometric construction skills and explores how shapes can fit together to cover a surface without gaps or overlaps. It connects geometric constructions with symmetry, angles, polygons, regular shapes and tiling patterns.

55 Questions: 15 MCQs + 10 True/False + 5 Assertion & Reasoning + 5 Fill in the Blanks + 5 Match the Following + 5 Drag & Drop Style + 10 HOTS.
Section A — Multiple Choice Questions (15)

1. Which instrument is primarily used to draw a circle with a specified radius?

Correct Answer: Compass

2. Which instrument is most directly used to measure an angle?

Correct Answer: Protractor

3. To construct the perpendicular bisector of a line segment, the compass should be opened to a distance:

Correct Answer: Greater than half the segment. This allows arcs from both endpoints to intersect.

4. A perpendicular bisector divides a line segment into:

Correct Answer: Two equal parts at right angles.

5. Which polygon can tile a plane by repeating congruent copies without gaps or overlaps?

Correct Answer: Square

6. The angle formed by two perpendicular lines is:

Correct Answer: 90°

7. Which regular polygon can tile the plane by itself?

Correct Answer: Equilateral triangle.

8. What must happen at every meeting point in a proper tiling?

Correct Answer: The angles around the point must add to 360°.

9. A regular hexagon has each interior angle equal to:

Correct Answer: 120°

10. Which condition is essential for a collection of shapes to form a tiling?

Correct Answer: There must be no gaps or overlaps.

11. Which construction creates a line through a point that is perpendicular to a given line?

Correct Answer: Perpendicular construction.

12. Which pair of regular polygons cannot individually tile the plane by repeating just one type?

Correct Answer: Regular pentagons.

13. If a point is equidistant from the two endpoints of a segment, it lies on the:

Correct Answer: Perpendicular bisector of the segment.

14. A regular triangle has each interior angle equal to:

Correct Answer: 60°

15. Why is a circle not able to tile a plane by itself using congruent circles?

Correct Answer: Gaps remain between circles.

26. Assertion: The perpendicular bisector of a segment passes through its midpoint.
Reason: A perpendicular bisector divides a segment into two equal parts.

Correct Answer: A

27. Assertion: A square can tile a plane.
Reason: Four right angles around a meeting point add to 360°.

Correct Answer: A

28. Assertion: Regular pentagons cannot form a simple edge-to-edge tiling by themselves.
Reason: Five regular pentagons meeting at one point would require 540° of angle.

Correct Answer: A. A regular pentagon has 108° at each vertex, and 5 × 108° = 540°, which is more than 360°.

29. Assertion: A compass is useful in geometric constructions.
Reason: A compass can transfer a length from one location to another.

Correct Answer: A

30. Assertion: Every polygon can tile a plane using congruent copies of itself.
Reason: Some polygons leave unavoidable gaps when repeated in a simple identical pattern.

Correct Answer: C. The Assertion is false, while the Reason is true.

Section G — HOTS / Higher Order Thinking (10)

46. A regular hexagon has six angles of 120°. Three hexagons meet at one vertex. Explain why this arrangement can tile the plane.

Correct Answer: 3 × 120° = 360°.

47. A student constructs a perpendicular bisector but opens the compass to less than half the segment length. What problem will occur?

Correct Answer: The arcs may fail to intersect, so the construction cannot be completed properly.

48. Can regular pentagons tile the plane by themselves in the usual edge-to-edge manner? Use their interior angle to decide.

Correct Answer: No. Three pentagons give 324°, leaving 36°; four give more than 360°.

49. A square tile is replaced by an identical square tile whose side length is doubled. What happens to the angle at each corner?

Correct Answer: It remains 90°. Changing side length does not change the angles of a square.

50. A point P is equidistant from A and B. A student claims that P must be the midpoint of AB. Is the claim correct?

Correct Answer: No. Every point on the perpendicular bisector is equidistant from A and B.

51. A regular polygon has an interior angle of 120°. Which polygon is it?

Correct Answer: Regular hexagon.

52. A designer wants a floor pattern made only from one type of regular polygon. The polygon must have an interior angle that divides 360° exactly. Which option is suitable?

Correct Answer: Regular hexagon, because 360 ÷ 120 = 3.

53. Two circles of the same radius touch externally. A third identical circle is placed between them so that it touches both. Why can this process not create a gap-free tiling using only these circles?

Correct Answer: Curved boundaries leave gaps between groups of congruent circles.

54. A student wants to construct a segment equal in length to a given segment AB without measuring its numerical length. Which method is best?

Correct Answer: Use a compass to transfer the length AB.

55. Three different regular polygons meet at one point: an equilateral triangle, a square and a regular hexagon. Their angles are 60°, 90° and 120°. What is special about this combination?

Correct Answer: 60° + 90° + 120° = 270°, so a 90° gap remains.
